## Purpose

Some accounts are worth more than a standard invite. The White Glove program identifies roughly [PLACEHOLDER] 50-75 global accounts where Oktane attendance could materially move a strategic deal, protect a large renewal, or produce a reference customer — and gives them a concierge experience: named exec sponsor, travel and visa support, pre-booked executive meetings, and a curated agenda.

For international customers this matters more than it does domestically. A CISO flying [PLACEHOLDER] 14+ hours from Singapore or navigating a visa process from São Paulo is making a real commitment. The White Glove program makes that commitment feel justified before they land, and makes sure the on-site experience pays it off.

This is a small list on purpose. Every account added dilutes the concierge promise for the rest.

## How to use this

1. **Nominate.** If a Tier 1 account (see Strategy 1) has strategic weight — [PLACEHOLDER] $XXXk+ opportunity, competitive displacement, flagship reference potential — submit it via the nomination form with a two-sentence justification. Regional leads approve.
2. **If your account is accepted:** you own the relationship, the program owns the logistics. Build the attendance plan with your account team: which customer contacts should attend (aim for an exec plus a practitioner), which exec meetings to request, which sessions map to their initiatives.
3. **Make the invite personal.** White Glove accounts should never receive the standard invite email. The first touch should come from the exec sponsor or from you referencing their specific initiative.
4. **Start travel logistics immediately on acceptance.** The program provides [PLACEHOLDER] visa invitation letters, itinerary support, and possibly travel assistance — details TBD — but none of it compresses government visa timelines.
5. **If your nomination is declined**, the account stays in your Tier 1 motion with standard resources. Declined does not mean deprioritized.

:::region emea
For EMEA White Glove accounts, check works-council and corporate travel-policy constraints before the exec sponsor makes the invite. In several markets ([PLACEHOLDER] Germany, France, Nordics), an invitation that arrives before internal approval exists can create friction with the customer's own policies. Ask your champion how travel approvals work at their company as part of the first conversation, not after.
:::
