## Purpose

For an international attendee, the pass is the smallest line item. The real cost conversation is pass + long-haul airfare + [PLACEHOLDER] 4-5 hotel nights + time out of office, and that total is what their leadership approves or rejects. This strategy gives reps (a) accurate pricing and discount mechanics, and (b) a total-cost framing that makes the number defensible rather than surprising.

Rule one: never quote a price you haven't verified against the current registration page. Everything below is structural guidance with placeholder figures.

## How to use this

1. Lead with total cost honestly. A LATAM or APJ attendee's all-in cost may be [PLACEHOLDER] $5,000-$8,000. Naming the real number and then justifying it beats letting the customer discover it and feel misled.
2. Frame against alternatives, not against zero. Compare the all-in cost to: one enterprise training course for one person; a single day of the consulting they'd otherwise buy to answer the same architecture questions; the cost of a wrong platform decision on an initiative they're already funding. Three days of roadmap access, hands-on labs, and direct access to product leadership is cheap against any of those.
3. Stack every discount that applies before presenting a number: early-bird plus group rate plus any [PLACEHOLDER] account-team code. Presenting the best defensible number first avoids a second approval round at their company.
4. Push the group rate on multi-attendee accounts. Sending [PLACEHOLDER] 3 people at the group rate often costs less per useful outcome than sending 1 — parallel tracks mean coverage, and a delegation survives one person's calendar conflict.
5. Offer the justification letter template proactively. Champions frequently want to attend but don't know how to ask; handing them a pre-written business case removes their hardest step.
6. ROI talking points for the approval conversation: access to product roadmap sessions that shape next year's architecture decisions; face time with Okta engineering and executives that would otherwise take months of meeting requests to assemble; certification and lab hours that count as professional development budget, not just travel budget; peer benchmarking with organizations facing the same regulatory and scale problems.

:::region latam
LATAM budgets are typically more constrained and more exchange-rate sensitive — a US-dollar price can swing meaningfully in local-currency terms between approval and payment. Tactics: anchor on early-bird pricing and register early to lock the dollar amount; ask about [PLACEHOLDER] regional promo codes or LATAM customer-appreciation allocations before quoting list; suggest bundling the trip with other US meetings (HQ visit, partner meetings) so the airfare amortizes across multiple justifications; and raise the visa timeline in the same conversation as budget — an approved budget with a missed visa window is a wasted approval.
:::

:::region emea
EMEA travel approvals often route through formal travel policies and, in some countries, works-council norms — the blocker is frequently process, not money. Provide the justification letter early so it can enter the approval workflow. Sustainability is a live concern for many EMEA companies: acknowledge the long-haul flight honestly and point to consolidation framing — one trip replacing multiple US trips (roadmap briefing, exec meetings, training) has a defensible footprint story. :::
