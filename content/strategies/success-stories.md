## Purpose

The strongest answer to "is this trip worth it" is what happened for people like you who went. This strategy assembles an evidence library of prior international attendee outcomes: deals that accelerated after exec meetings, architectures unblocked by a lab or roadmap session, champions promoted internally after presenting what they learned, and expansion conversations that started at a networking dinner.

Every story in the library must be cleared for external use and stripped of anything not approved. Reps should never improvise customer references — use only what's in the library, exactly as approved.

## How to use this

1. Match the story to the customer: same region or same vertical beats a bigger-name mismatch. A [PLACEHOLDER] mid-market LATAM bank story does more for a LATAM prospect than a famous US enterprise story.
2. Use stories to answer the unspoken question behind objections. "Too expensive" is answered by an ROI story; "we'll watch online" is answered by a story about something that only happened in person.
3. Tell the story in three beats: situation (what they were working on), what happened at Oktane (specific — a session, a meeting, a conversation), outcome (what changed afterward). Keep it under a minute spoken.
4. If you have a customer with a good outcome from a past Oktane, submit it via the intake form. The library only grows if the field feeds it.
5. Never name a customer that isn't in the approved library, even casually ("companies like [X] attend"). Use anonymized versions instead: "[PLACEHOLDER] customer example — a regional healthcare provider in EMEA."

:::region apj
APJ prospects respond strongly to peer proof from their own market — a Japanese customer story carries far more weight in Tokyo than any US reference, and the same holds for Australia, India, and Korea individually; APJ is not one market. Prioritize collecting per-country stories, and note that some APJ customers ([PLACEHOLDER] especially in Japan) may approve anonymized stories more readily than named ones. Lead with the anonymized ask if the named ask stalls.
:::

:::region pubsec
Public sector stories need extra care: outcomes framed as "accelerated procurement" or "influenced a deal" can create ethics-optics problems for the agency named. Frame PubSec stories around mission and capability outcomes — modernization milestones, zero trust progress, staff enablement — and get [PLACEHOLDER] agency public-affairs sign-off in addition to standard approval before any named use.
:::
