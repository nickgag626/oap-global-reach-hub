## Purpose

This is the master value narrative every other strategy draws from. When a customer, their manager, or their finance team asks "why should we spend money and three days on this," the answer needs to be concrete, honest, and specific to what happens in person. This page holds that answer.

The core argument: Oktane compresses months of vendor engagement into three days. The things attendees report as most valuable are precisely the things that don't happen remotely — unscripted roadmap conversations under NDA, hands-on labs with engineers in the room, exec meetings that would take a quarter to schedule otherwise, and hallway conversations with peers running the same architecture at the same scale.

## How to use this

1. Match value to persona before the call. A CISO cares about roadmap certainty, peer benchmarking, and exec access; an architect cares about labs, deep-dive sessions, and getting unstuck on specific designs; an admin cares about certifications and practical sessions they can apply the week they return. Pitch the persona, not the event.
2. Use the "compression" frame explicitly: list what the customer currently wants from us (roadmap briefing, architecture review, exec conversation, training) and show that all of it happens in one trip. This turns the conference from an extra cost into a consolidation of costs they were going to incur anyway.
3. Be honest about what's streamed. Keynotes and many breakouts will be available online. Credibility on this point is what makes the in-person-only list persuasive — see the digital-attendance objection page for the full structure.
4. Anchor ROI in a decision they already face: "You're deciding [PLACEHOLDER] your CIAM architecture this year. Three days that de-risk that decision is the cheapest insurance available on a [PLACEHOLDER] seven-figure project."
5. For multi-attendee pushes, use the coverage argument: parallel tracks mean one person physically cannot capture the event; two or three people with a divide-and-conquer plan multiply the return on largely fixed trip logistics.
6. After the customer agrees in principle, immediately hand them the justification letter template (Strategy 7) — momentum dies in their internal approval queue, not on your call.

:::region pubsec
Public sector value framing must respect procurement and ethics rules. Never offer to pay for or subsidize a government employee's travel or pass without explicit clearance — gift rules in most jurisdictions treat that as a violation, and even the offer can damage trust. Frame value strictly as professional development and mission capability: zero trust mandate progress, workforce modernization, compliance readiness ([PLACEHOLDER] FedRAMP/StateRAMP session references TBD). Many agencies can pay their own way from training budgets if the content is framed as training — the [PLACEHOLDER] certification and lab hours are the strongest lever. Timing note: US federal fiscal year ends September 30; a September 22-24 event sits inside year-end spend for some agencies and dead-zone for others — ask, don't assume.
:::
